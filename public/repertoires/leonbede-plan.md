# Opening foundations

A personal repertoire prepared for Leon Bede from a public Chess.com snapshot dated 2026-10-30. The sample contains 1106 standard games, including 130 rapid games. Results do not identify the phase in which a mistake occurred.

White starts with 1.d4. Against 1.e4, the Sicilian Dragon and ...e5 are separate alternatives. Against d4, choose ...d5/Albin or a King’s Indian setup.

## Slav and Semi-Slav

Distinguish ...c6, ...e6 and an early ...dxc4.

- Development and the d-file support a central break. After ...c5, count attackers and defenders before taking a pawn.
- Balance pressure on b7 with the defence of c3 and king safety. Check the opposing bishop’s diagonal before placing a rook on it.
- Central breaks and sacrifices in the Meran require calculation. Checks and material recovery take priority over general rules.

## Other defences to 1.d4

Englund, Indian defences, Benoni, Benko and the Dutch.

- Develop with tempo against an early queen and check every capture. Do not castle mechanically when a tactic is available.
- Keep the space advantage, but secure the king first. This is an alternative, not proof that 2.d5 must be abandoned.
- Qc2 belongs to the classical system. Respond to ...d5 according to central tension; the bishop pair is not the only goal.

## Sicilian Dragon

Yugoslav Attack, Soltis, classical setups and the ...d5 break.

- Start with development, castling and the c-file. Then choose between the Soltis setup and a concrete exchange on d4.
- ...h5 belongs to a specific Soltis structure. Calculate tempos, c-file pressure and exchanges against a kingside attack.
- Without Bc4, a central break can transform the position. Do not copy a flank pawn race blindly.

## Anti-Sicilians

Alapin, Moscow, Grand Prix and early Bc4 systems.

- Calculate central threats before quiet development. Capturing e4 requires concrete justification.

## Open games with 1…e5

Italian, Ruy Lopez, Scotch, Vienna and gambit responses.

- Coordinate pieces to prepare ...d5. Reassess every piece’s activity when the centre opens.
- Calculate the response to White’s central attack. Check ...Nxe4 before routinely exchanging bishops.
- Black receives activity for the pawn. Learn the concrete move order.

## Fianchetto against d4, c4 and Nf3

King’s Indian structures, London, Colle, English and Réti transpositions.

- A closed centre allows flank play, but White’s c5 needs a concrete response. Balance the attack with queenside defence.
- This is an intentional gambit approach with active pieces. Understand the compensation before memorizing it.
- Prioritize central activity and knight squares rather than copying the attack from a closed centre.

## 1…d5: Albin and queen’s pawn systems

Albin, London, Colle, Veresov and early e4 responses.

- The Albin is a deliberately riskier gambit. Prioritize development and activity over an unsupported flank attack.
- Seek central compensation and active squares. The gambit does not guarantee equality and should not depend on a trap.
- White has declined the gambit. Switch to development and central tension.
